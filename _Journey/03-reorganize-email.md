# Step 3: Reorganize Your Email

**Prompt:** Coming soon. Interim manual checklist below.

## Why This Order

A clean inbox unblocks your day. Cowork can deliver a meaningful daily briefing, suggest replies, and surface what needs your attention only after the inbox represents reality, not three weeks of accumulated noise.

Doing this after [Step 2](02-clean-onedrive.md) matters because email triage often produces files (attachments worth keeping, decisions worth documenting) that land in OneDrive. A clean OneDrive gives those files a place to go.

## Checklist

- [ ] In a fresh Cowork chat, ask it to triage your inbox in this order: archive done items, surface needs-response, schedule calendarables, delete noise.
- [ ] Approve actions in batches. Cowork pauses for medium and high risk actions.
- [ ] Have Cowork suggest folder or category rules for recurring senders or topics (newsletters, calendar invites, internal announcements, vendor notifications).
- [ ] Set up a daily briefing scheduled prompt: *"Send me a daily briefing every weekday at 8 AM covering yesterday's done items, today's meetings, and three top priorities."*
- [ ] Confirm the briefing appears the next morning. If not, check the **Scheduled** tab in the Cowork side panel.
- [ ] Spot-check inbox after one week. If new email piles up faster than Cowork's rules handle it, refine the rules.

## The Daily Briefing Pattern

The daily briefing is the highest-leverage scheduled prompt for most people. A good briefing answers three questions before you open your laptop:

1. What happened yesterday that I should know?
2. What is on my calendar today?
3. What are the three most important things I should focus on?

A weak briefing reads like a calendar export. A strong briefing has opinions: "You have three meetings stacked back-to-back from 2 to 4. Consider moving the 3 PM if the 2 PM is the harder conversation."

You will not get the strong version on day one. Tune the prompt over a week or two until the briefing actually helps you.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Do not let Cowork mass-delete.** Cowork can move emails into folders but cannot permanently delete them across the board. Use Archive or a quarantine folder for "probably noise" items. Review weekly until your rules are tight.
- **Avoid over-categorizing.** If you create 15 folders, you will spend more time deciding where each email goes than reading them. Three to five top-level folders is plenty for most people.
- **Watch for triage that misclassifies high-stakes senders.** Your manager's email landing in "newsletters" is a real risk. Spot-check the first week of automated triage.
